## Who I am

I’m a Northwestern student studying Mechanical Engineering and Neuroscience because I’m drawn to problems that sit between people, biology, and design. Across my work, the common thread is building rigorous, practical systems that can improve care. Whether I’m working on neuromodulation, orthopedic sensing, or clinical software, I’m most interested in translating technical ideas into tools that matter in the real world.

## What I care about

I care about giving patients and clinicians better tools: tools that are thoughtful, useful, and grounded in real clinical needs. That is what pulls me toward both research and startups, especially in medical devices, neuroscience, and translational engineering.

I also care about expanding access to opportunity and support. Through the DreamPrint Initiative, I work with underserved elementary students by providing 3D-printed models of their career aspirations while teaching basic design skills ([click here to learn more](http://www.tinyurl.com/dpinitiative)). Through The MindWell Project, I work on dementia and traumatic brain injury awareness, brain health education, and cognitive support access ([click here to learn more](https://tinyurl.com/mindwellproj)).

More broadly, I care about responsible innovation, teaching myself hard things, and building with people who raise the bar.

## What I’m building toward

I want to work at the intersection of research, medicine, and product development. My long-term goal is to help lead the translation of medical and neurotechnology devices from idea to real-world use, whether through founding ventures or leading technical development. Put simply, I want to build technologies that make care better and bring strong ideas out of the lab and into people’s lives.

## A few personal details

Outside of classes and projects, I enjoy reading, 3D printing, and building LEGO Star Wars sets. I like work that combines big ideas with clarity, and I’ve learned a lot from designs that looked good on paper but failed under real constraints. Those moments have made me a better builder. I also value community, service, and making things that feel tangible and useful.
